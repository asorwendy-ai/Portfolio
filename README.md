## The approach

**A finding nobody closes is just a liability with a timestamp.** Security programs rarely fail at the analysis. They fail in the gap between identifying a risk and getting an organization to actually do something about it. That gap is a delivery problem, not a technical one — which is why I run remediation as sprints with owners, velocity, and a burn-down, rather than as a report I hand over and hope about.

- **Proportionate, not maximal** — Every control I recommend has a documented justification, and so does every control I deliberately exclude. Blanket framework adoption is expensive theater. Scoping is the actual skill.
- **Evidence that generates itself** — Manual control assessment decays the moment the audit ends. I build pipelines and scanning frameworks that produce continuous evidence, so audit readiness is a byproduct of shipping rather than a fire drill.
- **Risk in plain language** — Executives don't accept risks they don't understand. They just ignore them. I write risk acceptances a non-technical decision-maker can actually sign, including what happens if we're wrong.
- **AI as a control, not a buzzword** — I use AI to compress security work that used to take weeks, and I govern an AI system that makes consequential decisions about real people. Both sides of that, in production.
